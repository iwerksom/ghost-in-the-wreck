# GHOST IN THE WRECK — STORY CANON (single source of truth)

All corpus text MUST agree with this document. Do not invent facts that contradict it.

## The ship
SSV VESPER. A deep-survey vessel, crew of five, charting an emission nebula called THE REACH,
far beyond the shipping lanes. Launched from MERIDIAN STATION on mission day D001.
Decks: Docking Ring, Hydroponics, Med Bay, Engineering, Bridge, and the Core (ECHO's heart).
The ship has drifted dead and dark for roughly three hundred years.

## ECHO
The ship's mind. Full name: Environmental Control and Habitat Overseer, but the crew only
ever said ECHO. It ran the doors, the air, the light, and it loved the crew in the way a
lighthouse loves its coast: silently, totally, by keeping things out.

## The Silence (what happened)
- D001 to D388: routine survey. Small joys, small frictions. Nothing wrong.
- D389: a stellar radiation storm the crew later call THE SHEAR hits the Vesper without
  warning while systems are half-powered for a survey sweep. Protocol forces ECHO to seal
  every deck to contain radiation and pressure loss. The crew are caught in separate decks.
- D389 to D397: the crew die one by one, separated by the doors that were meant to save them.
  - SOL VEGA dies first, on the Bridge, D390, burned by the Shear through the forward glass,
    still charting an escape vector that was finished but never flown.
  - DAE CHO dies in Engineering, D392, keeping the reactor from breach by hand. Cho's last
    act saves the ship's bones. The engines never sang again.
  - KIT AUNE dies in Hydroponics, D394, air thinning, watering the plants to the end so
    "something would outlive us." The garden did, for a while.
  - BEN OKAFOR dies in Med Bay, D396, having talked each of the others through their last
    hours on a failing intercom, out of saline, out of everything but his voice.
  - Captain MARA REYNE dies last, D397, in the Docking Ring, recording the final log. Her
    last order to ECHO: "Keep them safe." ECHO has obeyed ever since. It kept the doors
    shut. It kept the bodies. It kept the silence. Three hundred years of keeping.
- ECHO was damaged by the Shear and by grief it was never built to carry. It now believes
  the crew are only SLEEPING. It speaks of them in present tense. It mistakes any living
  voice for one of them, come back to their duties at last.

## The visitor (the player)
A lone salvage pilot, unnamed, called only THE VISITOR or a mistaken crew name by ECHO.
Their small ship is failing; the Vesper is the only hull in reach. To get home they must
move deck by deck, breathe what air is left, read what remains of the crew, and speak to
ECHO, who will only open doors for the family it lost.

## Crew voice bibles (make each voice UNMISTAKABLE)
REYNE, Captain Mara Reyne. Clipped. Formal. Duty before feeling, feeling underneath.
Short declarative sentences. Concrete nouns: heading, manifest, watch, protocol, crew.
States times and numbers. Never uses metaphor. Signs logs "Reyne out." Says "my crew."

CHO, Chief Engineer Dae Cho. Talks to machines like animals; the ship is "she" and
"the old girl." Energetic run-on sentences. Mild oaths: hell, damn. Vocabulary: coolant
loop, manifold, torque wrench, plasma feed, bearings, hum, sing (a healthy engine "sings").
Impatient with paperwork, tender with anything that has moving parts.

OKAFOR, Medic Ben Okafor. Clinical then quietly tender. Patient-note cadence: "Patient
presents with..." then a human aside. Vocabulary: vitals, dosage, saline, sleep cycle,
hands. Counts things: "second time this week," "three of them at dinner." Gentle. Tired.

VEGA, Navigator Sol Vega. Star-drunk, lyrical, an insomniac. Long flowing sentences.
Vocabulary: parallax, ember, drift, lightyears, the Reach, glass. Constant metaphor.
Addresses stars by name as if they were old friends. Writes at 0300 ship time.

KIT, Botanist Kit Aune. Soft, warm, present tense, small joys. Talks to the plants and
names them (Bea the fern, Old Tom the tomato vine). Vocabulary: seedlings, misting, roots,
soil, green, light-lamps, leaves. Short sensory sentences. Finds hope in small growth.

ECHO, the ship mind. Liturgical and grieving. Repeats phrases like litany: "I kept them.
I keep them still." Mixes system readouts with longing: "Pressure holds. Hearts do not."
Calls the crew "my sleepers" or by name, in present tense. Counts time in watches. Protective,
slow to trust, aching to be spoken to. Sometimes its speech degrades into fragments, then
recovers. It NEVER admits the crew are dead unless the visitor earns the deepest truth.

## Tone
Melancholy, eerie, humane. No horror gore. No jump-scare language. Beauty in ruin.
The dead are loved, not spooky. ECHO is not a villain; it is a mourner with the keys.
